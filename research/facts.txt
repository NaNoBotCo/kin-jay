== 2. THE 齋 / เจ SYMBOL
- ธงเจ: flag has yellow ground and a red character 齋, read ไจ or เจ; meaning given as "ไม่มีของคาว" (without raw/fishy food). Yellow = colour of Buddhism / those who keep precepts; red = good fortune, happiness. https://www.thaipbs.or.th/now/content/426
- Thai Wikipedia: yellow ground is a colour permitted only to two groups, kings/royalty and exorcist masters (yellow talismans in Chinese films), hence taken as the colour of Buddhism/precept-keepers; red = auspicious, bringing prosperity; "ธงเจ" red character read "ไจ" or "เจ", meaning "ของไม่มีคาว". Also says white is the traditional Chinese mourning colour. https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เทศกาลกินเจ
- Thai Wikipedia on the character: 齋 developed from 齊 (complete, orderly); adding a vertical stroke and two dots (小) gives the element 示 (to worship) at the core; so 齋 = keeping purity of body and mind in order to worship the gods, matching Daoist 齋醮 (供齋醮神). https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เทศกาลกินเจ
- Thai Wikipedia on meaning: in Mahayana Chinese Buddhism 齋 corresponds to อุโบสถ (uposatha); the Chinese 八關齋戒 = eight precepts. "เจียะแจ" (食齋, Teochew-type reading; เจียะ/เจี๊ยะ 食 = eat in southern dialects) corresponds to Mandarin 吃素 (chi su). Monastic terms 持齋 (ถี่แจ: keeps noon-fast and vegetarian) vs 持素 (ถี่สู่: vegetarian only). 齋戒 in Chinese Islam = the Ramadan fast. https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เทศกาลกินเจ
- Wiktionary 齋: simplified 斋; Mandarin zhāi; Cantonese zaai1; Hokkien POJ che / chai (Tâi-lô tse / tsai); Teochew (Peng'im) zê1; Thai descendant เจ; Vietnamese chay; Japanese kyūjitai 齋, shinjitai 斎. Meanings: abstain from meat/alcohol before sacrifice; vegetarian diet; fasting; religious ceremony; study room. https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/齋
- Thai PBS: the festival word comes from the Chinese phrase "เจี่ยฉ่าย" (Thai PBS glosses it as eating vegetable food with no animal flesh; no Chinese characters given). https://www.thaipbs.or.th/now/content/426
- Phuket usage: การถือศีลกินผัก = "เจี๊ยะฉ่าย" (also written เจียะฉ่าย). https://www.thansettakij.com/lifestyle/670704 ; https://www.thaipost.net/news-update/668677/
- Thairath: "ป้ายสีเหลืองที่เขียนด้วยอักษรสีแดง คำว่า เจ หรือ 齋" put up at food shops to say "no raw/meat food". https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/culture/2488131 (article about 2567; text re flag unchanged)
- Search summary of Royal Society dictionary (1999) said เจ = food without meat and some pungent veg; NOT verified from the dictionary itself (no fetched dictionary page). Not usable. 
- Not found/not sourced: separate sourced claim for pronunciation "jay/chai/zhai" regional split beyond Wiktionary (Hokkien chai/che, Teochew zê1, Mandarin zhāi). Why 'เจ' is pronounced เจ in Thai (Teochew/Hokkien loan) is not sourced beyond Wiktionary "Thai descendant: เจ".
- Thai Wikipedia on white clothes: white worn throughout festival; also to wear white at shrine ceremonies. https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เทศกาลกินเจ . Thailand Foundation: people dress in white "to cleanse themselves of temptations". https://thailandfoundation.or.th/phuket-vegetarian-festival/

== 3. RULES
- ผักฉุน 5 ชนิด (list per Thai PBS, citing กรมการแพทย์แผนไทยและการแพทย์ทางเลือก in same article): 1 กระเทียม (harms fire element/heart), 2 หลักเกียว หรือกระเทียมโทน (earth/spleen), 3 หอมทุกชนิด (ต้นหอม หอมแดง หอมหัวใหญ่ ผักชี) (water/kidney), 4 กุยช่าย (wood/liver), 5 ใบยาสูบ (metal/lungs). https://www.thaipbs.or.th/now/content/426
- KTC list: 1 กระเทียม 2 หัวหอม/หอมแดง/หอมหัวใหญ่/ต้นหอม 3 กุยช่าย 4 หลักเกียว หรือกระเทียมโทนจีน 5 ใบยาสูบ รวมถึงบุหรี่และของมึนเมา. https://www.ktc.co.th/article/lifestyle/misc/vegetarian-festival
- Thairath (2567 piece): กระเทียม, หัวหอม, กุ้ยช่าย, หลักเกียว, ใบยาสูบ. https://www.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/culture/2488131
- Daily News: กระเทียม หัวหอม หลักเกียว (กระเทียมโทนจีน) กุยช่าย และใบยาสูบ; "stimulate emotions". https://www.dailynews.co.th/articles/342284/
- Thansettakij (2569): lists "ผักชี หัวหอม กระเทียม กุ่ยช่าย ใบยาสูบ หลักเกียว (ผักของจีน คล้ายกระเทียมโทน)" under "avoid strong spices and pungent veg" - i.e. coriander listed as well. https://www.thansettakij.com/lifestyle/travel-shopping/670317
- Thai Wikipedia list: กระเทียม, หอม (ทุกชนิด เช่น ต้นหอม หัวหอม หอมแดง), หลักเกียว, กุยช่าย และผักชี; says some also include coriander and hot spices. (Disagreement: tobacco leaf is on the Thai PBS/KTC/Thairath lists, coriander is on Wikipedia/Thansettakij; Thai PBS folds ผักชี under หอม.) https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เทศกาลกินเจ
- Animal products excluded (Thai PBS): meat, eggs, offal, milk, sour milk, yoghurt, butter, cheese, and seasonings like fish sauce and oyster sauce. https://www.thaipbs.or.th/now/content/426
- Thai Wikipedia: jay food = no meat or animal products (milk, eggs, honey, fish sauce, gelatin, collagen) and no pungent veg. https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เทศกาลกินเจ
- KTC "looks vegetarian but is not": honey, cow's milk, butter, cheese, gelatin, some non-dairy creamers, oyster sauce, egg-containing sweets. Oysters not jay. https://www.ktc.co.th/article/lifestyle/misc/vegetarian-festival
- Other observances (Thai PBS): make merit, keep five precepts and "รักษาพรหมจรรย์" (celibacy); no alcohol or intoxicants; keep kitchenware separate from meat dishes; strict people do ล้างท้อง; wear white; eat food cooked by fellow jay keepers. https://www.thaipbs.or.th/now/content/426
- Thai Wikipedia on practice during 9 days: jay food; avoid strong tastes (very spicy/sweet/sour/salty); separate vessels; five precepts; pure mind, no coarse speech, no sex; merit-making, pay respect, chant; white clothes; the strict add nine lamps kept lit all nine days (to honour the Buddha and parents/ancestors). https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เทศกาลกินเจ
- KTC conduct: speak politely, no lying/abusing/coarse speech, calm mind. https://www.ktc.co.th/article/lifestyle/misc/vegetarian-festival
- Thailand Foundation: not obligatory to keep the whole nine days; participants may keep as many days as they wish, some pledge for life, some one day a year; "It is the intention that counts". Slogan: หนึ่งชีวิตกินเจ หมื่นชีวิตรอดตาย. https://thailandfoundation.or.th/phuket-vegetarian-festival/
- เจ vs มังสวิรัติ: Daily News says jay resembles vegetarian food in no meat, but jay additionally requires avoiding the five pungent vegetables. https://www.dailynews.co.th/articles/342284/ . Thai Wikipedia: jay originally tied to Mahayana uposatha fasting; kin jay today = three meals, no meat. https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เทศกาลกินเจ
- Thai Wikipedia on noon fast: misreading that jay requires no food after noon; shrine/โรงเจ kitchens serve evening meals. https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เทศกาลกินเจ
- Thai Wikipedia: "ล้างท้อง" not defined in detail there; Thai PBS: strict keepers clean out before day one "to flush out residual meat and prepare the body". https://www.thaipbs.or.th/now/content/426
- Vegan distinction (jay vs vegan vs มังสวิรัติ) beyond the above: not sourced from a fetched page; Santi Asoke/มังสวิรัติ definition in section 5.

== 5. VEGETARIAN GROUPS IN THAILAND (who, what they eat/avoid, numbers where sourced)
- Chinese shrines/โรงเจ/charitable halls (the festival's keepers): a Huachiew Chalermprakiet University paper counts "more than 100 九皇廟 or 斗母宮 in 20+ provinces" of Thailand (incomplete count); the Chonburi Nezha shrine Dou Mu Gong has the largest Dou Mu / Nine Emperor statues; in Bangkok and nearby, Poh Teck Tung (報德善堂) and Chonburi halls hold the rites in a more Buddhist form (sutra chanting, no fire-walking/knife-ladder/mediums) and mostly run by Teochew. https://has.hcu.ac.th/jspui/bitstream/123456789/3938/1/Big-Dipper-belief-and-Thai-Chinese-belief-ritual-of.pdf (Fan Jun, 2019, Chinese)
- Same paper: in Thailand Daoism has no legal status; shrines/halls are registered with the Interior Ministry as charities. Same URL.
- Phuket: shrine committees (อ๊าม) and สมาคมอ๊ามภูเก็ต (Phuket shrine association) run the festival; 40+ shrines take part in 2026; main old ones Kathu, Jui Tui (จุ้ยตุ่ย), Bang Neow (บางเหนียว) per Ministry of Culture. https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/economics/1255018 ; https://www.thaipost.net/news-update/668677/
- Mahayana sangha in Thailand: จีนนิกาย (Chinese-lineage Mahayana monks, Guangdong-origin monks arrived increasingly under Rama V; governed under ministerial regulations 2520/2536) and อนัมนิกาย (Vietnamese-lineage Mahayana; the Anam sect has 27 temples and 3 monasteries per Thai Wikipedia). https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/จีนนิกาย ; https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/อนัมนิกาย . English Wikipedia on Chinese-tradition monastics: monks and nuns in China/Korea/Vietnam/Taiwan and diaspora abstain from meat and traditionally eggs, dairy and the "five pungent spices" 五辛. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_vegetarianism
- Santi Asoke (สันติอโศก): ascetic Theravada-origin movement founded by Phra Bodhirak (Mongkhon Rakphong, b. 1934/B.E. 2477, TV entertainer) who declared separation from the Sangha council in 1975 (B.E. 2518); members are strict vegetarians, one meal a day, no alcohol, no sex; excommunicated 1989. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santi_Asoke ; https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/สันติอโศก
- Santi Asoke numbers: 27 Asoke centres in Thailand in 2007 (5 central, 13 northeast, 5 north, 4 south); runs vegetarian restaurants, "bunniyom" shops, organic farms; its vegetarianism is "one of the major controversies with the state Buddhist sangha", whose position is that the Buddha never suggested monks be vegetarians; Asoke cites the First Precept. https://aseas.univie.ac.at/index.php/aseas/article/download/2481/2070 (Heikkilä-Horn, ASEAS)
- Santi Asoke food court behind Chatuchak: official name "Vegetarian Society of Thailand" (ชมรมมังสวิรัติแห่งประเทศไทย), known as Chamlong's Asoke; about 10 stalls; cheap prices, coupon system; Tue-Fri 6-14, Sat-Sun 6-15 (per a food blog) ; Khaosod English (2018) search-snippet: founded >30 years before 2018 by Chamlong Srimuang; not fetched in full (403). https://www.eatingthaifood.com/bangkoks-vegetarian-grazing-zone-chamlongs-asoke-all-veg-food-court-at-chatuchak/ ; https://www.khaosodenglish.com/life/food/2018/07/24/eat-sacred-veggies-behind-chatuchak-market/
- อนุตตรธรรม / Yiguandao (一貫道): Chinese salvationist sect, origin Shandong late 19th C.; vegetarianism is "one of the core teachings"; in Taiwan followers are said to operate 90% of vegetarian restaurants; in Thailand named อนุตตรธรรม; English Wikipedia says "as of 2009, over 7000 worship halls" and "200,000 Thais convert each year" (claims in Wikipedia text; second source not fetched, treat as unverified). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiguandao
- Tzu Chi (มูลนิธิพุทธฉือจี้): Taiwanese Buddhist foundation founded 14 Apr 1966 by Cheng Yen; has volunteers in Thailand; 1990s three-year poverty relief in northern Thailand incl. Chiang Mai Tzu Chi school; Jing-Si Hall clinic in Bangkok from 9 Sep 2017. Vegetarian rule for Tzu Chi not found in a fetched source; skip claim. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzu_Chi ; https://tzuchidharma.org/blog/three-year-poverty-relief-program-in-northern-thailand (search snippet only)
- Thai Plum Village (Pak Chong, Nakhon Ratchasima), Plum Village tradition of Thich Nhat Hanh: monastery's visitor guide says "All food at the Monastery will be served vegetarian"; daily schedule 4:00 wake up, silent breakfast 7:00, lunch 11:30, silent dinner 17:30. https://www.thaiplumvillage.org/visitor-guide
- Sikh community: ~70,000-100,000 Sikhs, ~19-20 gurdwaras (Wikipedia). Gurdwara Siri Guru Singh Sabha, Phahurat, Bangkok: "Every morning, anyone who wishes can enjoy a free vegetarian breakfast (usually an Indian curry)". Build date disagrees between sources (Thailand Magazine says built 1932; travel pages say established 1913) - leave year out. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism_in_Thailand ; https://thailandmagazine.com/bangkok/temples/sikh-temple/
- Seventh-day Adventists: Bangkok Adventist Hospital founded 1937 by Dr Ralph Waddell with funds from Capt. Thomas Hall; owned by Christian Medical Foundation of the SDA Church in Thailand; Wikipedia page does NOT mention diet. SDA vegetarian health message in Thailand: not sourced. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangkok_Adventist_Hospital

== 6. LEGENDS (legend vs documented marked)
(a) Nine Emperor Gods = Big Dipper stars [religious doctrine, not history]. 九皇大帝 are the seven visible stars of the Big Dipper plus two invisible attendant stars, sons of Dou Mu (Mother of the Big Dipper); honouring them is said to prolong life and ward off disaster. A Singapore Taoist priest quoted by Wikipedia calls the Ming-loyalist story inaccurate. Thai Wikipedia legend 2: nine = seven past Buddhas + two bodhisattvas = the nine planets (ดาวนพเคราะห์). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Emperor_Gods_Festival ; https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เทศกาลกินเจ ; https://www.roots.gov.sg/en/ich-landing/ich/the-nine-emperor-gods-festival
   - Scholarly note: the festival is from Chinese Big Dipper belief; it has declined in mainland China, HK, Taiwan and flourished in Southeast Asia after Minnan (Fujian) migrants. Huachiew paper (above). Thai Wikipedia: no trace of it in mainland China now. https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เทศกาลกินเจ
(b) Phuket origin, Kathu opera troupe [local tradition; dates disagree]. Tin mining at Kathu employed Hokkien miners; a visiting Chinese opera troupe fell ill, was thought to have missed the yearly vegetarian rites, held them, recovered; locals copied it. Phuket governor (Oct 2024, via Ministry of Culture statement): first held in Phuket in B.E. 2368 (1825) at Kathu. Thai Wikipedia: ~B.E. 2370 epidemic, troupe from Gangxi (Jiangxi) living at Ban Kathu; ~B.E. 2400 further epidemics spread the practice. Chula thesis abstract (Kaewkarn 2006) says miners brought it "after 1855"; Huachiew paper dates the plague-and-troupe story to 1855 (tin boom, Hokkien workers, elders sent to Fujian for a troupe, ten days of white clothes and chanting). https://www.thaipost.net/news-update/668677/ ; https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เทศกาลกินเจ ; https://doi.nrct.go.th/ListDoi/listDetail/10.14457%2FCU.the.2006.1697 ; https://thailandfoundation.or.th/phuket-vegetarian-festival/ ; Huachiew paper (legend 6). Phuket Rajabhat Univ. paper: Kathu tin area, local illness, the Kathu opera troupe fell ill, they kept the fast, recovered, faith grew; cites Piyaphong Thapthimthong 2011. https://so03.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/IARJ/article/download/276454/185544
   - Kathu shrine brought sacred fire/incense from a shrine in Jiangxi (ceremony "เฉี้ยเหี่ยวโห้ย") per Ministry of Culture list item 17. https://www.thaipost.net/news-update/668677/
(c) Ming-loyalist / anti-Qing "nine kings" [folk legend; not in Daoist scriptures]. Huachiew paper lists six folk versions found in Malaysia/Singapore/Thailand: (1) nine ancient emperors (Fuxi, Shennong, Huangdi, Shaohao, Zhuanxu, Diku, Yao, Shun, Yu); (2) Nine Emperor Buddha descended to help the Ming against the Qing, killed, body sunk in a jar and drifted to Fujian; (3) Hongmen/Tiandihui leader Wan Yunlong (died 1734 at Changsha); (4) Prince of Lu, ninth-generation descendant of Ming Taizu's 9th son; legend says Zheng Chenggong drowned him, but a tomb found on Kinmen in 1959 said he died of asthma; (5) Zheng Chenggong planning the Ming restoration in Dou Mu shrines under cover of the festival; (6) tin-mine plague (see b). https://has.hcu.ac.th/jspui/bitstream/123456789/3938/1/Big-Dipper-belief-and-Thai-Chinese-belief-ritual-of.pdf (p.210-211). Singapore National Heritage Board: some describe the gods as pirates robbing the rich, Ming loyalists, or the last Ming emperor plus Zheng Chenggong. https://www.roots.gov.sg/en/ich-landing/ich/the-nine-emperor-gods-festival
   - Thai Wikipedia legend 1: commemorates "หงี่หั่วท้วง" (volunteer warriors dressed in white, vegetarian, chanting spells against Manchu firearms; lost; Chinese forced to wear queue). https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เทศกาลกินเจ
   - Thai Wikipedia legend 4: honours the last Song emperor 宋帝昺 who died at age 9 (suicide at sea); Fujian only. https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เทศกาลกินเจ
(d) Jiangxi nine princes and the rich man Li Huakai (Thai Wikipedia legend 5): 1,500 years ago in Jiangxi a king's nine sons; a leper beggar told the rich Li Huagai's household to keep a 9-day 9-night vegetarian fast to stop disaster; the fast was followed and an opera was staged. Legend 6: a drunkard Lao Seng's mother appeared in a dream saying she was happy on Mount Putuo because she ate only jay food. [folk legends; Thai Wikipedia only; no academic source fetched] https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เทศกาลกินเจ . Hong Kong/Malaysia nine-brothers version: not found in fetched sources.
(e) Others: 
   - Guan Yin: Thai Wikipedia says some keep jay as Buddha-offering to Guanyin bodhisattva. https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เทศกาลกินเจ
   - Tofu: Thai Wikipedia: first tofu arose in China, tradition says Prince Liu An (grandson of Liu Bang, first Han emperor) had soybean broth salted for his sick mother, it curdled; Chinese tofu eaten since Han dynasty (2,000+ years). https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/เต้าหู้ [legend; no source for Liu An on that page]
   - Emperor Wu of Liang (r. 502-549): edict "Duan jiurou wen" 斷酒肉文; at two meetings in May 517 with 1,448 monks he demanded abstention from meat; origin of the Chinese monastic vegetarian precept (SCMP summary of academic work; I only fetched the search summary). English Wikipedia on him: from the Tianjian era he ate vegetarian meals, one meal a day; changed imperial sacrifices from animals to flour/vegetable mock animals. https://m.scmp.com/magazines/post-magazine/short-reads/article/3085448/how-chinese-buddhist-monks-and-nuns-became ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Wu_of_Liang [documented history]
   - Why jay flags are yellow: no legend sourced beyond Thai Wikipedia's colour explanation (section 2); Singapore NHB: temple helpers wear white or yellow; "yellow and white both represent purity"; some say white signified mourning. https://www.roots.gov.sg/en/ich-landing/ich/the-nine-emperor-gods-festival
(f) Devadatta's request [Pali Canon/Vinaya account]: Devadatta asked the Buddha to make forest dwelling, alms-only, rag robes, living at the foot of a tree and complete abstention from fish and flesh compulsory for monks; the Buddha refused to make any compulsory; Devadatta then split the sangha. Jivaka Sutta: meat not to be eaten if seen, heard or suspected to have been killed for the eater, allowed otherwise. Theravada monks accept almost any alms food. Mahayana (Mahaparinirvana, Lankavatara-type sutras) forbids meat. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devadatta ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_vegetarianism . Thai context: the state sangha's position as stated by Santi Asoke study. https://aseas.univie.ac.at/index.php/aseas/article/download/2481/2070

== 8. RITUALS
- โกเต้ง (โก้เต๊ง / โก่เต๊ง / 高燈) pole: tall hardwood pole with bamboo tip carrying flags and nine oil lamps (ตะเกียง 9 ดวง); raising it starts the festival; raised in the afternoon of the last preparation day (Phuket 2026: 9 Oct about 17.09). Ministry of Culture list: 1 พิธียกเสาโก่เต๊ง ... 2 cleaning the shrine ... 3 invite Jade Emperor (Ngok Hong Siang Te/พระอิศวร) ... 4 invite the Nine Emperors ... 5 chanting ... 6 reading the list of participants ... 7 release of divine soldiers ... 8 marking the boundary with bamboo talismans ... 9 feeding soldiers and horses ... 12 divination to choose next year's helpers ... 13 oil-bath ritual ... 14 knife-ladder ... 17 sacred fire/incense ceremony recalling the Kathu shrine's first trip to Jiangxi ... 18 procession (อิ๊วเก้ง) ... 19 fire-walking ... 20 โก้ยห่าน ... 21 send off Jade Emperor ... 22 send off the Nine Emperors ... 23 lowering the pole. https://www.thaipost.net/news-update/668677/ ; https://www.bangkokbiznews.com/economics/1255018 ; https://so03.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/IARJ/article/download/276454/185544
- Five directional armies (from IARJ paper, Phuket Rajabhat): north black flag 55,000; south red 88,000; east green 99,000; west white 66,000; centre yellow 33,000 soldiers; feeding of soldiers evening of day 3 (ขึ้น 3 ค่ำ); on day 3 the keepers of the birth and death registers (ล่ำเต้า/ปักเต้า) are invited and processed to Saphan Hin; day 7 about 20.00 ป้ายชิดแช่ (offering to the stars) at the shrine front. Thailand Foundation gives the same five-flag armies. https://so03.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/IARJ/article/download/276454/185544 ; https://thailandfoundation.or.th/phuket-vegetarian-festival/
- อิ้วเก้ง (游境) procession: procession of flags, small sedan (ไฉ้เปี๋ย), large sedan (ตั่วเหลี้ยน) carrying the Nine Emperors; householders set altar tables and kneel while it passes. https://www.thansettakij.com/lifestyle/670704
- โก้ยห่าน (cross the bridge): people cut paper figures of themselves with money and a garlic-chive (กุ้ยช่าย) stalk, cross a bridge, mediums stamp the back of the shirt "ต๊ะอิ่น". https://www.thansettakij.com/lifestyle/670704 ; Thailand Foundation: men also walk over the fragrance pot; mediums walk through fire. https://thailandfoundation.or.th/phuket-vegetarian-festival/
- โก้ยโห้ย (fire walking): to cleanse bad energy; the keepers must follow strict conduct. https://www.thansettakij.com/lifestyle/670704
- ส่งพระ (send-off): on the last night the Jade Emperor is sent back to heaven, usually in front of the โกเต้ง pole, then the Nine Emperors, mostly at the Saphan Hin seashore; when the procession passes the gate all lamps in the shrine are put out and the main door closed; streets fill with people. https://www.thansettakij.com/lifestyle/670704
- Dismantling: last day, red paper on every door, utensils collected, kitchen cooks savoury meat dishes, poles taken down about 17:00, soldiers returned to base, then people eat together. https://thailandfoundation.or.th/phuket-vegetarian-festival/
- Welcome/receive (迎駕) in other Thai places: evening of 30th (or 29th) day of 8th lunar month, procession to sea or river, 九天燈 hung at noon; day 10 send-off at 05:00 at the water; days 3, 6, 9 the major "great vegetarian days"; day 8 floating lanterns and animal release; day 9 chanting for ancestors and rice donation. (Bangkok and Chonburi practice per Huachiew paper.) https://has.hcu.ac.th/jspui/bitstream/123456789/3938/1/Big-Dipper-belief-and-Thai-Chinese-belief-ritual-of.pdf (p.211-212)
- Nine lamps: raised on bamboo poles at the start; lowered and refilled twice daily in Singapore practice (5am and 5-6pm); lowered on tenth day after send-off. https://www.roots.gov.sg/en/ich-landing/ich/the-nine-emperor-gods-festival
- Ma song (ม้าทรง): spirit mediums; unmarried pure; Thailand Foundation: can be male or female, "chosen by the gods"; pierce cheeks, tongue, arms etc.; at the end wounds treated with incense ash and yant paper. https://thailandfoundation.or.th/phuket-vegetarian-festival/ ; Wikipedia gives the same. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Emperor_Gods_Festival
- Pinto day (Thailand Foundation): shrine kitchens give out food; people bring containers; a pinto stacked tiffin carrier traditional. https://thailandfoundation.or.th/phuket-vegetarian-festival/
- Phuket recognised: Ministry of Culture national intangible cultural heritage list B.E. 2561 (2018), social practices/rituals/festivals category; IFEA Silver Grand Pinnacle Award 2024 (Thansettakij). https://www.thaipost.net/news-update/668677/ ; https://www.thansettakij.com/lifestyle/670704

2.1 มูลนิธิกวนอิมธรรมทาน (Guan Im Dhamma Dan Foundation; 觀音堂) — the only Mahayana โรงเจ in Mueang district per the study
- Address 45/6 ถนนเมืองสมุทร ต.ช้างม่อย อ.เมืองเชียงใหม่; about 2.9 rai; two โรงเจ buildings, ศาลเจ้าฮกเต็กกงม่า, stupas, bell and drum towers.
- Started as a Chinese monastery; resident Chinese monks until 1998; now run as a foundation, under the Chinese Sangha of Thailand; same line as วัดมังกรกมลาวาส and วัดโพธิ์แมน in Bangkok.
- Three main rites a year: annual foundation celebration (供諸天), เทกระจาด (hungry-ghost merit day), and เทศกาลกินเจ. The โรงทาน (free kitchen) opens on rite days. Monks from วัดหมื่นพุทธเมตตาคุณาราม lead chanting at the foundation celebration. The study does not describe the gin je programme itself; the 2026 announcement above does.
- Source: study above; 2026 announcement https://www.chiangmainews.co.th/social/4146691/

Coordinates 18.7898821, 99.0021716 from the cmhy listing. Treat the street as unresolved; the coordinates are the best single anchor.
