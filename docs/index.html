Read the flag: 齋 · เจ

Hang the yellow flag and you are telling the street: nothing from an animal in this food. Chinese signs write 齋. Thai signs spell it เจ.

Tap to replay.

Readingsjay in Thai, chai in Hokkien, zhāi in Mandarin, chay in Vietnamese; simplified Chinese 斋
Yellowonce kept for royalty and exorcists, so read as the colour of Buddhism and the precepts (Thai Wikipedia)
Redgood fortune
Whitewhat keepers wear

Jay or not? Cook it.

No meat, fish, egg, milk, honey, fish sauce or oyster sauce. No pungent five, either: that is the line between jay and vegetarian.

Tap ingredients into the wok.

Garlicกระเทียม

AlsoNo alcohol. The strict cook in pots that have never held meat, keep the five precepts, keep their speech gentle and abstain from sex.

Who eats jay

Shrine hallsโรงเจ · ศาลเจ้า · อ๊าม

They run the festival. Over a hundred Nine Emperor and Dou Mu temples in 20+ provinces, registered as charities.

Source
Mahayana monksจีนนิกาย · อนัมนิกาย

Chinese-lineage monks skip meat, egg, dairy and the pungent five. The Vietnamese-lineage Annam Nikaya: 27 temples, 3 monasteries.

Source
Santi Asokeสันติอโศก

Phra Bodhirak's movement, apart from the Sangha council since 1975. Strict vegetarian, one meal a day, 27 centres (2007), five in the north.

Source
Yiguandaoอนุตตรธรรม · 一貫道

A Chinese religion from 1800s Shandong. Vegetarian eating is a core teaching.

Source
Thai Plum Villageหมู่บ้านพลัม

Thich Nhat Hanh's monastery at Pak Chong. Every meal vegetarian.

Source
Sikh gurdwarasคุรุดวารา

About twenty in Thailand. Phahurat's Siri Guru Singh Sabha feeds anyone a free vegetarian breakfast; Chiang Mai's dates from 1907.

Source
Theravada laypeopleฆราวาส

A sizeable minority eat vegetarian, Thailand and Sri Lanka above all.

Legends

Join the Dipper

Tap the seven stars in order, bowl to handle.

The nine starsdoctrine

The Nine Emperor Gods are the Big Dipper's seven stars plus two no one can see, sons of Dou Mu. Honour them, live longer.

The Thai telling: seven past Buddhas and two bodhisattvas, the nine planets.

Source
The opera troupe at Kathulocal tradition

Fever hits the tin mines of Kathu, Phuket. A Chinese opera troupe falls sick, blames its skipped vegetarian rites, keeps them, and recovers. The town follows.

1825, says the governor; 1827, says Thai Wikipedia; after 1855, says a Chula thesis.

Source
The Ming loyalistsfolk legend

White-clad fighters eat no meat or pungent plants, chant spells against Manchu guns, and lose. The festival remembers them.

A Huachiew study finds six versions across Thailand, Malaysia and Singapore.

Source
The nine princes of Jiangxifolk legend

A leper beggar at rich Li Huakai's gate orders nine days of jay to stop a coming disaster. Li obeys, the town follows, the disaster passes.

Source
Lao Seng's motherfolk legend

A drunk dreams of his dead mother: she is happy on Mount Putuo because she ate only jay. He sets out, breaks his vow on the road, and still finds her floating above the incense pot.

Source
Emperor Wu of Lianghistory

From 502 the Liang emperor eats one vegetarian meal a day and swaps the goats, pigs and cattle of the imperial sacrifice for animals moulded from flour.

Source
Devadatta's five rulesPali canon

Devadatta asks the Buddha to ban fish and meat for monks. The Buddha refuses; Devadatta splits the Sangha.

Source

Make tofu

Legend credits Liu An, grandson of the first Han emperor: salted soy broth for his sick mother set into curds.

Words

เจjay · 齋

Food with nothing from an animal and none of the five pungent plants; also the observance itself.

กินเจkin jay · 食齋

To keep jay. In Phuket the older word is chia chai, 'eat vegetables'.

ล้างท้องlang thong

The clean-out day before the nine days.

ออกเจok jay

Coming out of jay after the last night.

โรงเจrong jay

A jay hall: a Chinese religious hall with its own kitchen.

อ๊ามam · 庵

Phuket's word for a shrine.

โกเต้งko teng · 高燈

The tall lantern pole raised to begin the festival, carrying the nine lamps.

กิ๋วอ๋องไต่เต่kiu ong tai te · 九皇大帝

The Nine Emperor Gods.

ม้าทรงma song

A spirit medium, 'the horse the god rides'.

อิ้วเก้งiu keng · 游境

The procession that carries the gods through the streets.

Fire-walking.

โก้ยห่านkoi han

Crossing the bridge: a rite of cleansing at the shrine.

The five pungent plants jay leaves out.
